How Shininess is Determined

Determining shininess works differently in gens 1 & 2 than in gens 3+. Rather than using the player's trainer ID, secret ID, and the Pokémon's personality value, Pokémon are shiny when they have a specific DV spread. Specifically a Pokémon must have 10 speed, defense, and special DVs, and 2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11, 14, or 15 attack DVs. You may also hear people say that shiny Pokémon need either 0 or 8 HP DVs, but this is only partially true and a misconception with how DVs are calculated. The HP DV is calculated based on the other DVs, meaning that while every shiny Pokémon will have 0 or 8, it's not technically a requirement for a Pokémon to be shiny.

As gen 2 was the first generation with shiny Pokémon, Game Freak didn't purposefully implement a shiny hunting method into the game. Because of this many gen 2 hunts are full odds (1/8192).

Quirks

Using DVs to determine shininess introduced some quirks when interacting with other attributes and mechanics that also used DVs.

  • Pokemon with a 7:1 gender ratio may not be simultaneously shiny and female. e.g. shiny starters can only be male.
    • For a brief time poke transporter had a bug which randomly determined gender rather than using the Attack DV of a Pokémon. This meant that shiny females from gens 1 & 2 with a 7:1 gender ratio were briefly possible from the virtual console games. This bug was fixed in version 1.3
  • Only Unown I and V can be shiny. (this is a pretty funny coincidence considering DVs were replaced with IVs in gen 3)
  • Shiny Pokémon can only have a hidden power of Grass or Dragon with a power of 49 or 69.
  • Two shiny Pokémon cannot breed because Pokémon with the same Defense DV cannot breed.

Method 0 - Guaranteed Shinies

Red Gyarados is the only fixed shiny encounter in the gen 2 games. This encounter happens in the Lake of Rage as part of the story and you will not have another opportunity to catch it if you faint it. Catching this Gyarados is important if you intend to breed for shinies (see Method 3 below.), whether you breed with Gyarados directly or use it catch a shiny ditto in the Gen 1 games.

Method 1 - Random Encounters

Roaming legendary DVs are locked in when you first encounter them. This makes them an SR hunt and not a RE hunt.

Almost no setup is required to do random encounter hunts. Simply find where the Pokémon you want to hunt appears in the wild and encounter Pokémon until it shines. Make sure to bring a catching Pokémon whenever possible and enough pokéballs to catch it.

Method 2 - Soft Resetting

You can soft reset for any gift or static Pokémon. The most obvious examples of this are the starters and static legendaries. To hunt them just save in front of the Pokémon (or who/what gives you the Pokémon) and reset the game until it shines. Make sure you set your text speed to fast to speed up text boxes. Also bring a catching Pokémon and pokéballs if applicable.

Starter Hunt Optimizations

There are a few extra things you can do to optimize the soft reset hunts for starters. None of the optimizations save a huge amount of time on their own, but when used together and over the course of thousands of resets they will save you a *lot* of time.

  1. Play GS over C - Pokémon Crystal has more dialogue during this hunt. If you aren't dead set on playing it consider hunting in Gold or Silver instead.
  2. Stand to the left of the table for Cyndaquil - If you're hunting Cyndaquil you can stand to the left of the table allowing you to skip the walk animation to the professor. This saves a few seconds per reset.
  3. Check their gender on the nickname screen - Normally you would want to skip the nickname screen entirely, however in generation 2 Pokémon with a 7:1 gender ratio cannot simultaneously be the minor gender (in this case female) and shiny. Checking the gender on the nickname screen allows you to reset ~20 seconds early for ~1/8 of your resets. Since checking the screen only loses ~1.2 seconds on resets with a male starter, this will save you time as long as ~1/16 of the resets has a female which will almost surely happen, especially over the full length of your hunt.
  4. Reset if the HP stat isn't right - Shiny Pokémon in gen 2 will always have an HP DV of 0 or 8. Because you can briefly see the HP of your Pokémon before checking it's summary to see if it's shiny, you can reset the game ~1 second early if you reset when the HP value doesn't match one of the values corresponding to your starter in the table below.
Pokémon 0 HP DV 8 HP DV

Method 3 - Shiny Gene Breeding

Shiny breeding in gen 2 works because DVs are passed down from one parent (the opposite gender parent or Ditto) and as discussed at the beginning of this page, DVs determine if a Pokémon is shiny.

Odds

The exact odds will vary when not using ditto to carry shiny genes as the gender of the child (and therefor the parent passing down DVs) is random and two Pokémon with the ideal "shiny genes" cannot breed since they have the same defense stat.

When breeding Pokémon with a 7:1 gender ratio (using a female shiny gene parent) or using ditto, the odds are 1/64.

How DVs are passed down

DVs are generated when the egg is created and are passed down from the opposite gender parent or Ditto if you're using one (Ditto will always take priority.)

  • The attack value is randomly generated. It also determines the gender of the Pokémon which is important for the generation of other DVs when not using Ditto.
  • The defense value is passed down from the parent.
  • The special value is passed down from the parent, but 50% of the time it will have an offset of 8. E.g. a parent with a Special DV of 12 could either pass down 12 or 4 to the child.
  • The speed DV is determined randomly.
  • The health DV is determined by the other stats

The Perfect Parent (Shiny Genes and Ditto)

To get the most out of shiny breeding you need a parent Pokémon with 10 defense DVs and 10 or 2 special DVs since those are the stats getting passed down. Any shiny Pokémon will meet this criteria but you could also just catch any Pokémon and check these two DVs.

Ditto is the most useful parent since it can breed with any Pokémon trivializing this whole process and actually gives better odds in most scenarios. A guide for obtaining a good ditto for breeding (with and without using glitches) can be found in my useful Pokémon page.

Method 4 - Odd Egg

The Odd Egg only exists in Pokemon Crystal, this section does not apply to GS players

The Japanese version of Crystal required you to interact with the Mobile Game Boy Adapter service to receive the Odd Egg, as such this method doesn't (legitimately) work on the Japanese version of crystal anymore, original or 3DS VC.

Explanation & Odds

The Odd Egg is pretty straight forward: You can get one per save file, it can hatch into any of the baby pokemon introduced in Gen 2, and there is a 14% chance of the hatched pokemon being shiny. The odds of each possible pokemon being shiny, however, are not uniform; below is a table with the odds that each pokemon is hatched from the egg, normal and shiny. To hunt using the Odd egg simply save before obtaining it, hatch it, and reset if you don't get the pokemon you want.
